## Complete proof route

1. The private measure-extensionality theorem concerns the actual subtype BridgeNoise of continuous paths pinned at zero and one. The continuous-map Borel measurable structure is generated by evaluations; the subtype measurable structure is the pullback of that structure. The product-function measurable structure is the supremum of evaluation pullbacks. Thus the BridgeNoise measurable space is exactly the pullback along its map to all point evaluations. A measurable BridgeNoise set is the inverse image of a measurable function-space set. Equality of pushforward measures therefore gives equality on every measurable BridgeNoise set. This proof never assumes the range of continuous paths is a measurable subset of the uncountable product space.

2. For two actual standard IsBrownianReal processes, on possibly different probability spaces, use the qualified simultaneous almost-everywhere formula for bridgeNoise: g(t)=B(t)-t*B(1). For any finite collection of bridge times I, let J be the union of their nonnegative real times and time1. Native IsPreBrownianReal.hasLaw gives both J-vectors the same canonical standard Brownian projective-family law. Their measurable linear maps, subtracting t times the endpoint, are identically distributed. The simultaneous almost-everywhere bridge formula identifies these transformed vectors with the actual finite evaluation vectors of bridgeNoise. These evaluation maps are almost-everywhere measurable. Their function-space pushforwards are two projective limits of the same finite family, so cached IsProjectiveLimit.unique makes the function laws equal. The first theorem then identifies the actual BridgeNoise laws. No finite-dimensional identity is presented as the root theorem; the projective-limit and evaluation-generated measurable-structure steps explicitly close the passage to full path measures.

3. Native Brownian scaling, already paid by unit121, shows B(T*t)/sqrt(T) is standard when T>0. Apply the proved universality to obtain its bridge law equal to bridgeLaw B P. This is a theorem, not an added hypothesis.

4. The private physical-event equivalence uses the surjection t↦T*t from [0,1] to [0,T] for T>0. In the forward direction select t=s/T for s≤T. In the reverse direction T*t≤T. All events retain strict spatial survival and include both physical time endpoints. No assertion equates a pointwise strict event with a weaker closed-wall event.

5. Rewrite the qualified unit121 HasDerivAt theorem by the law equality and event equivalence. The resulting physical-time joint CDF is exactly P.real{ω | (∀s:NNReal,s≤T→|y+B(s,ω)|<a) ∧ y+B(T,ω)≤v}. Its derivative at z equals gaussianPDFReal y T z times intervalCompletion (bridgeLaw B P) a y T z, with arbitrary real a,y,z and T≠0.

6. The final theorem divides the actual derivative by the strictly positive physical free Gaussian density. This identifies the actual interval completion as that density ratio. There is no analytic image hypothesis in this density-ratio theorem, and it does not itself identify the density with the killed heat kernel or analytic image candidate.
