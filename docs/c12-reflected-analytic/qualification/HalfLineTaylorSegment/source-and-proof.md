Segment energy is T-theta*b^2, wall distance u-theta*b and increment profile exp(-max(u-|b|,0)*v/T), exactly the one-wall source P_b. Upper wall uses b as given; lower wall uses -b, with identical b^2 and |b|. No source convention is changed.

For 0<=theta<=1 and b^2<=T/2, energy lies in [T/2,T] and is positive. If u>0 and u-b>=0, wall distance is a convex combination of u and u-b: it is nonnegative at the full segment and strictly positive for theta<1. Thus a new point exactly on the wall is allowed. The source profile transport holds even at that endpoint. v>=0 suffices, a harmless strengthening of applicability beyond strict terminal interior.

The wall distance dominates max(u-|b|,0), while energy is at most T. Multiplication by v>=0 and positive division show the segment exponent is at most the source increment exponent.
