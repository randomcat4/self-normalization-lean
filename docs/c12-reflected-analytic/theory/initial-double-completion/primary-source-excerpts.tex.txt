\subsection{Initial double completion and the exact flags}
Use $g_x$ and its standard bridge $G$ from Lemma~4.4, and put
\[
 j_x(w)=P(\max_s(G_s+sw)\ge x,\min_s(G_s+sw)\le-x).
\]
\begin{lemma}[Initial completion]\label{d:initial}
For $x\ge1$,
$\|j_x\|_\infty+\operatorname{TV}(j_x)\le Ce^{-4x^2}$.
If $W=S_n/\sqrt Q$ on $Q>0$ and $W=0$ otherwise, and
$b_x=R_n(x)\le1/2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{d:initialbound}
 \left|E[1_{\{Q>0\}}j_x(W)+1_{\{Q=0\}}]-d(x)\right|
 \le Cb_x\Psi(x),\qquad x\ge1.
\end{equation}
The corresponding initial interval marginal and terminal-half-line
comparisons have absolute error $CR_n(0)$ for every $x>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Cancel the free and two nearest reflected images in
Lemma~\ref{cn:kernel}. On $|w|\le x$ the correct series is
\begin{equation}\label{d:jimages}
 j_x(w)=\sum_{m\ne0}e^{-8m^2x^2-4mxw}
 -\sum_{\substack{k\in2\mathbb Z+1\\|k|\ge3}}
                                      e^{-2k^2x^2-2kxw}.
\end{equation}
Each image is monotone in $w$, so its variation is at most its larger
endpoint value. The two summed bounds are
$2\sum_{m\ge1}e^{-4m(2m-1)x^2}$ and
$2\sum_{l\ge1}e^{-4l(2l+1)x^2}$, both at most $Ce^{-4x^2}$
for $x\ge1$. Outside, reflection gives
$j_x(w)=e^{-2x(x+|w|)}$; its two variations total $2e^{-4x^2}$.
The values join continuously at $\pm x$. Normal convergence justifies
the interior derivative integration and gives the supremum bound.

Stieltjes integration against the scalar estimate
\eqref{cn:scalar} costs at most variation times $CR_n(0)$.
Multiplication by $1_{\{w\le v\}}$ adds at most the supremum norm
to variation, also at an atom since the comparison normal cdf is
continuous. The Brownian endpoint/bridge decomposition identifies the
normal expectations. Removing $Q=0$ costs its probability times the
supremum. To pay its rare scale, set $\pi=P(X\ne0)$ and normalize
variance to one. If $b_x\le1/2$, the second moment truncated at
$\sqrt n/(1+x)$ is at least $1/2$. H\"older on its nonzero support
gives its third moment at least $1/(2\sqrt{2\pi})$. Thus
\[
 n\pi\ge\frac{(1+x)^6}{8b_x^2},\qquad
 P(Q=0)\le e^{-(1+x)^6/(8b_x^2)}\le Cb_x\Psi(x).
\]
For the last inequality use $(1+x)^6\ge2x^2+8$ and the lower Mills
bound; both $(1+x)e^{-x^2/2}$ and $b_x^{-1}e^{-1/b_x^2}$ are bounded.
This proves \eqref{d:initialbound}. The absolute interval assertions are Lemma~4.4.
